# Konsider Phase 3B — Lightweight screening of 84 criteria

**Date:** 24 July 2026
**Universe:** `stable_supported_v1` (91 countries)
**Normal Phase 3 probe threshold:** 82/91 (at least 90%)
**Scope:** Gate A screening only. This report does **not** approve sources, measure country coverage, or complete a licensing audit.

## Executive finding

The screen retains **42 criteria** for deeper research. The first two batches contain **15** criteria each. High-value legal mobility, tax and housing criteria remain in the research list despite weak apparent coverage because Phase 3A explicitly permits strategic study of central relocation gates.

- Finding-status counts: DEFERRED 5; DEFERRED_CITY_LAYER 14; DEFERRED_PROFILE_LAYER 10; SCREENED_LOW_FEASIBILITY 12; SCREENED_POSSIBLE 29; SCREENED_PROMISING 14.
- Natural-granularity counts: city/regional 26; national 34; preference-based 2; profile-derived 17; unsuitable independent 5.
- Priority counts: HIGH 15; MEDIUM 27; LOW 30; NO FURTHER WORK 12.
- The strongest apparent national-data candidates are C11, C29, C30, C48, C49, C53, C62, C66–C70 and selected education/health components.
- The clearest city-layer deferrals are housing, international schools, everyday crime, emergency care, water continuity, transit, flight connectivity, urban life, recreation and non-PM2.5 environmental quality.
- C79–C84 are profile calculations, not independent source searches. C24, C44, C46, C52 and C63 already overlap existing Konsider criteria.

## Fact versus estimate boundary

- **Verified facts:** criterion definitions come from the supplied search-space document; cited official/primary pages verify that named publisher/dataset families exist and support the specific scope, date or licensing fact stated in the evidence register.
- **Preliminary estimates:** every criterion-level fit judgment, C0–C4 comparability grade, 90%-coverage plausibility, coverage band, freshness expectation, licensing-uncertainty flag, granularity decision, status and priority.
- **Not performed:** row-by-row downloads, 91-country joins, current-policy legal coding, exact licence-chain review, commercial-use legal advice, or measured freshness validation.

## Ranked list for deeper research (42)

1. **C11 — Overall job-market opportunity** (HIGH; SCREENED_PROMISING) — Vacancies are not globally comparable, so the first version should stay with harmonised labour outcomes.
2. **C32 — Skilled-work visa accessibility** (HIGH; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — No global ready-made dataset; eligibility depends on occupation, salary, origin, sponsor and changing rules.
3. **C33 — Permanent-residency accessibility** (HIGH; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — PR eligibility is route-, origin- and family-dependent and needs maintained legal coding.
4. **C17 — Average earning potential** (HIGH; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — National averages mask occupation, tax and city-cost differences; net pay requires household scenarios.
5. **C21 — Personal income-tax burden** (HIGH; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — OECD's comparable effective-tax model covers only 38 countries; global extension requires maintained official-rule coding.
6. **C25 — Housing affordability** (HIGH; DEFERRED_CITY_LAYER) — High decision value justifies strategic research, but a national average is misleading and global city rent data are often commercial.
7. **C50 — Healthcare system capacity and quality** (HIGH; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — No single indicator captures access, quality and waiting time; mixed years and modelled values are likely.
8. **C40 — English usability** (HIGH; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Commercial proficiency rankings are not enough; usability varies strongly by city and domain.
9. **C01 — Overall higher-education opportunity** (HIGH; SCREENED_PROMISING) — Quality is not captured by availability alone; rankings are commercially licensed and institution-weighted.
10. **C30 — Existing immigrant share** (HIGH; SCREENED_PROMISING) — The source mixes country-of-birth and citizenship definitions and many 2024 values are extrapolated.
11. **C12 — Software and technology jobs** (HIGH; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Global occupation aggregates are broad; granular tech vacancies are platform-dependent and concentrated in cities.
12. **C58 — Internet access, speed, and reliability** (HIGH; SCREENED_PROMISING) — ITU access/coverage is broad, but speed/outage measures may be commercial or incomplete; avoid duplicating the existing composite.
13. **C56 — Electricity access and reliability** (HIGH; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Access is near-global, but outage metrics lack harmonised 90% coverage; current experimental composite overlaps access.
14. **C08 — School education quality** (HIGH; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Administrative completion is broad, but common learning assessments miss many stable-universe countries and cycles.
15. **C66 — Extreme-weather risk** (HIGH; SCREENED_PROMISING) — Hazard components have different scales and city exposure; avoid double counting vulnerability/coping capacity.
16. **C38 — Professional-licensing accessibility for immigrants** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Origin, profession, credential and language dependence prevent a universal score; deep research should test scenario templates.
17. **C35 — Post-study migration pathway** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Pathways depend on credential level, institution, occupation and policy date.
18. **C36 — Family reunification support** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Family definitions, income thresholds and sponsor status differ materially; parent sponsorship is especially non-comparable.
19. **C34 — Citizenship accessibility** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Formal law is codable, but discretion, residence counting and policy updates complicate a scalar score.
20. **C05 — Research and innovation ecosystem** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_PROMISING) — A composite must avoid mixing inputs and outputs or importing opaque GII weights.
21. **C15 — Engineering and skilled technical jobs** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Broad ISCO groups are comparable, but specialisms, licensing and regional projects are not.
22. **C13 — Medical and healthcare jobs** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Provider shortage is not equivalent to accessible jobs, and licensing must remain a separate gate.
23. **C14 — Business, finance, and professional-services jobs** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Occupational groupings are broad and vacancy data are uneven; major-city concentration is material.
24. **C16 — Entrepreneurship and startup opportunity** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Startup funding/activity sources often have selective coverage or commercial terms; formation counts are not survival quality.
25. **C19 — Employment protection and worker rights** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Law, ratification and enforcement are distinct; comparable coding requires current legal review.
26. **C22 — Social-security and mandatory contribution burden** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Global comparability is constrained by ceilings, benefit entitlements and the same OECD coverage limit.
27. **C26 — Healthcare affordability** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Aggregate out-of-pocket shares do not describe immigrant eligibility, premiums or point-of-care prices.
28. **C29 — Currency and macroeconomic stability** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_PROMISING) — A transparent small indicator set is preferable to a proprietary sovereign-risk composite.
29. **C48 — Political stability and civil peace** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_PROMISING) — WGI is perception-based and conflict-event data may have licensing or reporting asymmetries.
30. **C49 — Rule of law and institutional trust** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_PROMISING) — WGI is a composite of perceptions with margins of error; institutional trust is not identical to rule of law.
31. **C53 — Water quality and sanitation** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_PROMISING) — JMP access is broad, but continuity, taste/contaminants and migrant-city reliability are separate constructs.
32. **C57 — Water-supply reliability** (MEDIUM; DEFERRED_CITY_LAYER) — JMP access does not ensure 24/7 supply; utility-level continuity is not globally standardised.
33. **C62 — Digital-government readiness** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_PROMISING) — Composite editions and weights require sensitivity review; user experience can diverge from formal availability.
34. **C67 — Long-term climate-change exposure** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_PROMISING) — Results depend on scenario, horizon, model ensemble and population exposure assumptions.
35. **C68 — Natural-disaster risk** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_PROMISING) — Overlaps extreme-weather risk; geophysical hazards should be separable and composite weights tested.
36. **C70 — Climate resilience and adaptation readiness** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_PROMISING) — Readiness indexes are composite/modelled and overlap governance, infrastructure and disaster risk.
37. **C71 — Work-life balance** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Leave law is not take-up; commute is city-level and survey coverage is weak, so start with hours/leave components.
38. **C72 — Family friendliness** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — This is best as a transparent family profile assembled from approved components, not a new opaque dataset.
39. **C06 — International-student accessibility** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Mobility stock is comparable; admissions, English-taught programmes and visa rules need manual current-policy coding.
40. **C07 — Tuition affordability** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Fees vary by institution, programme, residency and scholarship; global harmonised international-student prices are unlikely.
41. **C42 — Social inclusion and acceptance of immigrants** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — Global surveys differ in years and samples, and small states are often absent; lived experience varies locally.
42. **C45 — LGBTQ+ legal and social inclusion** (MEDIUM; SCREENED_POSSIBLE) — ILGA has strong scope but its 2024 report is CC BY-NC; social acceptance/safety needs separate survey evidence.

## Criteria likely to require city-level treatment

- **C02 — Technology and engineering education:** Global field-of-study counts are plausible, but comparable programme quality and institution location are not.
- **C03 — Medical and health-sciences education:** Programme quality, recognition and seat access vary by profession and institution; WDOMS is not a quality ranking.
- **C04 — Business, commerce, and management education:** Accreditation and rankings cover a selective institution set and carry licensing and selection bias.
- **C07 — Tuition affordability:** Fees vary by institution, programme, residency and scholarship; global harmonised international-student prices are unlikely.
- **C09 — International-school availability:** A city-level institution inventory and commercial-directory licensing would be required.
- **C12 — Software and technology jobs:** Global occupation aggregates are broad; granular tech vacancies are platform-dependent and concentrated in cities.
- **C13 — Medical and healthcare jobs:** Provider shortage is not equivalent to accessible jobs, and licensing must remain a separate gate.
- **C14 — Business, finance, and professional-services jobs:** Occupational groupings are broad and vacancy data are uneven; major-city concentration is material.
- **C15 — Engineering and skilled technical jobs:** Broad ISCO groups are comparable, but specialisms, licensing and regional projects are not.
- **C17 — Average earning potential:** National averages mask occupation, tax and city-cost differences; net pay requires household scenarios.
- **C18 — Income growth and career mobility:** Promotion and mobility require longitudinal microdata that are not globally harmonised or broadly reusable.
- **C25 — Housing affordability:** High decision value justifies strategic research, but a national average is misleading and global city rent data are often commercial.
- **C27 — Childcare and family-cost burden:** Fees depend on age, city, income, subsidies and immigration status; OECD-style comparisons do not approach global coverage.
- **C31 — Immigrant community depth:** Origin-specific usefulness and city concentration make a single national diversity score weak.
- **C40 — English usability:** Commercial proficiency rankings are not enough; usability varies strongly by city and domain.
- **C43 — Cultural openness and diversity:** The construct mixes diversity, tolerance and freedom; direction and weights are contestable and city effects large.
- **C47 — Property-crime and everyday safety:** Recorded crime is dominated by reporting/legal differences; globally harmonised victim surveys and city coverage are sparse.
- **C51 — Emergency-care readiness:** Global emergency-care assessments are sparse and national averages cannot represent response time.
- **C55 — Mental-health support:** Provider counts do not measure affordability or stigma, and access is local with substantial missingness.
- **C57 — Water-supply reliability:** JMP access does not ensure 24/7 supply; utility-level continuity is not globally standardised.
- **C60 — Urban public transport:** Country scores are invalid; a fixed city universe and reusable feed policy are prerequisites.
- **C61 — International connectivity:** Route schedules are city/airport and origin-specific; comprehensive timetable data are usually commercial.
- **C64 — Temperature comfort:** Global data are excellent, but national averages are misleading and comfort has no universal direction.
- **C69 — Environmental quality beyond PM2.5:** The construct is too broad, city-sensitive and dependent on composite weighting; EPI licensing must be checked.
- **C73 — Quality of urban life:** Requires a city universe, geospatial methodology and multiple sources with uneven licensing.
- **C74 — Recreation, culture, and leisure:** Amenity value is preference-based, highly local and usually reliant on commercial or volunteered POI data.

- **E01.** UNESCO UIS describes itself as the official source of cross-nationally comparable education data; its browser offers APIs, bulk downloads, metadata, and a February 2026 release. [Primary source](https://databrowser.uis.unesco.org/resources) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E02.** ILOSTAT provides bulk/API-style access to harmonised labour indicators, dictionaries, metadata, and modelled estimates. [Primary source](https://ilostat.ilo.org/data/bulk/) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E03.** UN DESA International Migrant Stock 2024 covers 233 countries and areas for 1990–2024, but many 2024 values are extrapolations rather than full reassessments. [Primary source](https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/content/international-migrant-stock) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E04.** WHO Global Health Observatory exposes country and indicator data through an OData API. [Primary source](https://www.who.int/data/gho/info/gho-odata-api) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E05.** ITU DataHub contains hundreds of ICT indicators and data for nearly 200 economies, including access, use, coverage, affordability, and quality-of-service indicators. [Primary source](https://datahub.itu.int/) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E06.** WHO/UNICEF JMP's 2025 household WASH release reports estimates through 2024. [Primary source](https://washdata.org/reports/jmp-2025-wash-households) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E07.** World Bank WGI 2025 reports six annual governance dimensions for more than 200 economies through 2024 and publishes uncertainty; it is a perception-based composite. [Primary source](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/worldwide-governance-indicators) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E08.** World Bank WBL 2026 covers 190 economies, is current to 1 October 2025, and its catalog records CC BY 4.0. [Primary source](https://datacatalog.worldbank.org/search/dataset/0038489/women-business-and-the-law) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E09.** World Bank ICP 2021 has observed results for 176 economies plus imputed results for 19; it provides PPPs and price-level indexes. [Primary source](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/icp/data) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E10.** IMF WEO April 2026 includes inflation, unemployment, growth, fiscal and external indicators and is published twice yearly, with some country-series gaps. [Primary source](https://data.imf.org/Datasets/WEO) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E11.** OECD Taxing Wages 2026 reports effective tax wedges for 38 member countries and selected household cases, well below the 91-country universe. [Primary source](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/04/taxing-wages-2026_d1f39986/3a5169ef-en.pdf) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E12.** World Bank CCKP offers country, subnational, watershed and gridded climate data, including API access and World Bank dataset terms. [Primary source](https://climateknowledgeportal.worldbank.org/download-data) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E13.** Copernicus ERA5 is a globally complete, consistent reanalysis from 1940 onward with continuing updates. [Primary source](https://climate.copernicus.eu/climate-reanalysis) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E14.** EU/JRC INFORM Risk 2026 is openly downloadable with source data and calculation steps for global country risk. [Primary source](https://drmkc.jrc.ec.europa.eu/inform-index/INFORM-Risk/Results-and-data/moduleId/1782/id/453/controller/Admin/a) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E15.** ILO NATLEX/NORMLEX provide large official corpora of national labour and social-security legislation, but not a ready-made relocation score. [Primary source](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/home) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E16.** ILGA World's 2024 Laws on Us covers 11 LGBTQ+ legal categories but is licensed CC BY-NC 4.0. [Primary source](https://ilga.org/laws-on-us-report/) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E17.** World Happiness Report 2026 uses harmonised Gallup life-evaluation responses averaged over 2023–2025 and makes the published three-year averages downloadable. [Primary source](https://www.worldhappiness.report/data-sharing/) (checked 2026-07-24).
- **E18.** World Bank Logistics Performance Index 2023 is an official cross-country measure of trade-logistics speed and reliability. [Primary source](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2023/04/21/world-bank-releases-logistics-performance-index-2023) (checked 2026-07-24).
